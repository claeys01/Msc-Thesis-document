The computational cost of accurate, high-fidelity CFD remains a bottleneck for
turbulent flow simulations, and the time integration of the discretised
Navier--Stokes equations is, in most mesh-based solvers, the most expensive part
of the solver loop. This thesis investigates how effectively a Neural Ordinary
Differential Equation (NODE) can accelerate that time-integration step without
compromising the physical accuracy of the simulation.

To answer this, a hybrid framework is developed around the WaterLily.jl
solver~\cite{Weymouth2025}. A convolutional autoencoder provides the non-linear
compression: the encoder $\varphi_\theta$ maps the augmented flow state
$(\mathbf{u}, \boldsymbol{\mu}_0^\epsilon)$ onto a low-dimensional latent vector
$\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_z}$, and the decoder $\psi_\theta$ reconstructs the
velocity field from that latent state. The autoencoder is trained with a
physics-informed loss that combines an $L_1$ reconstruction term with divergence
and vorticity penalties, so that the compressed representation stays physically
consistent rather than merely visually faithful. A Neural ODE $f_\phi$ then learns
the latent dynamics, advancing $\mathbf{z}$ in time so that the latent rollout
replaces a sequence of conventional solver steps. The NODE is trained with multiple
shooting, which partitions the trajectory into shorter segments and thereby
stabilises learning over the long, chaotic rollouts needed to span several
vortex-shedding cycles -- a regime in which direct single-shooting training is prone
to gradient blow-up. Because a latent rollout is only trustworthy while it remains
close to the dynamics it was trained on, a non-parametric KNN out-of-distribution
monitor gates the loop at runtime: it measures the distance between the predicted
latent vector and its nearest neighbours in the encoded training set, and when that
distance exceeds a calibrated threshold $\tau$ it discards the prediction and hands
control back to WaterLily, which re-stabilises the flow before the rollout resumes.
When the monitor raises repeated flags, the NODE is retrained on the augmented
trajectory set so that the pipeline can follow the flow as it evolves. The framework
is developed and evaluated on two-dimensional flow past a circular cylinder at
$Re = 2500$, on $256^2$ and $512^2$ grids with a latent dimension $N_z = 16$.

The results show that the hybrid loop reproduces the quantities that matter most for
the flow. On the finer grid, the time-averaged drag is recovered to within $0.64\%$
over the accelerated windows, the mean-flow and Reynolds-stress fields correlate with
the reference at $\rho \geq 0.9966$, and only the lift fluctuation carries a visible
rollout error, reflecting a mild decay of its amplitude within each rollout. Once the
networks are trained, the latent rollout integrates the flow far more cheaply per step
than the reference solver, giving an inference-loop speedup of
$S_\text{loop} \approx 1.48$.

The central limitation is economic rather than physical. When the one-off cost of
training the networks is charged to the comparison, the effective speedup of a single
run drops to $S_\text{eff} \approx 0.07$, an order of magnitude slower than simply
running WaterLily over the same window. The cost of the framework is therefore
dominated by training, not by the accelerated integration: the inference loop is
already cheaper than the reference, and that margin widens as the grid is refined. The
parameter studies all point to the same underlying lever. Narrowing the latent
bottleneck, shortening the training schedule, or tightening the multiple-shooting group
each simplifies the dynamics the NODE must learn and so lengthens the average rollout,
but at the cost of coarsening the reconstructed turbulence statistics; speed and
physical fidelity are thus traded against one another, and the rollout monitor sets
where on that trade the loop operates. The fixed training cost can be recovered by
reusing a trained pipeline across comparable runs, by trimming the training schedule --
which alone lifts $S_\text{eff}$ to $0.62$ on the finer grid -- or by overlapping
training with the live simulation.

This work therefore demonstrates that latent Neural ODE rollouts can replace part of
the conventional time integration without sacrificing the accuracy of the integrated
loading or the wake statistics, and identifies the training cost, rather than the
inference loop, as the bottleneck that any practical deployment of the method must
overcome.
